Q1.1 Define Digital Marketing and explain the POEM (Paid, Owned, Earned Media) Framework with a neat labelled diagram, comparison table, and an Indian brand example. 6 Marks • SPPU End-Sem
SPPU Marking Split: Formal Definitions (1M) · POEM Converged Diagram (2M) · Comparison Table & Explanation (2M) · Indian Brand Case & Takeaways (1M)

1. Definition / Core Principle

Digital Marketing is the measurable promotion of brands, products, or services through internet-connected electronic channels (search engines, social platforms, websites, mobile apps, and email) to attract, engage, convert, and retain target audiences in real time.

The POEM Framework classifies every digital customer touchpoint into three strategic pillars: Paid Media (sponsored visibility bought via ad spend), Owned Media (brand-controlled digital properties), and Earned Media (organic word-of-mouth, shares, reviews, and editorial backlinks).

2. Neat Labelled Diagram & Exam Explanation

Diagram Explanation (How to Describe in Exam)
  • Three Overlapping Circles: Paid Media buys immediate traffic (left), Owned Media acts as the permanent conversion hub (right), and Earned Media provides organic social proof (bottom).
  • Pairwise Intersections: Paid + Owned powers website retargeting; Owned + Earned drives high-ranking SEO content; Paid + Earned amplifies viral creator posts via Spark/Partnership ads.
  • Converged Synergy (Center): Maximum ROI occurs when Paid traffic lands on fast Owned pages whose superior UX sparks Earned reviews and shares.

3. Detailed POEM Comparison Matrix

Evaluation Criterion Paid Media Owned Media Earned Media
Brand Control Medium (subject to ad policies & auction rules) 100% Full Control (UI, copy, pricing, data) Zero Direct Control (driven by users & editors)
Cost Structure Direct recurring ad spend (CPC / CPM / CPA) Fixed engineering, hosting & content cost Rs. 0 direct media fee (earned via quality & PR)
Speed & Scalability Immediate (traffic starts within hours) Slow build (requires 3–12 months of SEO/CRM) Unpredictable, but can compound exponentially
Audience Trust Lower (users recognize "Sponsored" tag) Medium-High (professional brand credibility) Highest (peer recommendations & unbiased reviews)
Shelf Life Stops immediately when ad budget is paused Long-term compounding asset owned by brand Persistent digital footprint (search reviews, links)

4. Indian Brand Case Example & Exam Takeaways

  • Zomato POEM Flywheel: Paid (YouTube & IPL hotstar pre-roll ads) drives app installs; Owned (Zomato App + push notifications + Gold membership) captures UPI food orders; Earned (viral topical X/Twitter memes & restaurant ratings) lowers future acquisition cost.
  • Key Takeaway: Never rely solely on Paid Media ("rented land"); use Paid channels to grow Owned email/app audiences and spark Earned advocacy.
Q1.2 Distinguish between Traditional Marketing and Digital Marketing across eight operational, analytical, and strategic parameters. 5 Marks • SPPU Model QB
SPPU Marking Split: Core Distinction & Shift (1M) · 8-Point Structured Comparison Table (3M) · Practical Hybrid Scenario & Takeaways (1M)

1. Definition / Core Principle

Traditional Marketing relies on offline, one-way broadcast channels (print, television, radio, billboards) targeting broad geographic segments, whereas Digital Marketing leverages interactive, internet-enabled channels for two-way communication, hyper-granular audience targeting, and deterministic real-time ROI attribution.

2. Eight-Point SPPU Comparison Table

Dimension Traditional Marketing Digital Marketing
1. Primary Channels Print newspapers, TV commercials, FM radio, highway hoardings, pamphlets. Google Search (SEO/PPC), Instagram/LinkedIn, YouTube, Email, Websites, OTT.
2. Communication Flow One-way monologue (Brand → Passive Consumer); delayed feedback. Two-way interactive dialogue (Comments, DMs, live chat, instant reviews).
3. Audience Targeting Broad demographic/geographic guesses (e.g., all readers of a city daily). Hyper-granular targeting (Age, pin code, job title, purchase intent, pixel behavior).
4. Measurability & Attribution Imprecise estimates via TRP ratings, coupon codes, or post-campaign surveys. Exact real-time telemetry (Impressions, CTR, CPC, CPA, ROAS via GA4 & Pixels).
5. Budget & Entry Barrier High upfront minimum investment (lakhs for TV/hoarding production & slots). Accessible micro-budgets starting at Rs. 100/day with pay-for-performance bidding.
6. Campaign Agility Rigid; once printed or aired, typos, pricing, or creatives cannot be edited. Instantaneous; pause underperforming ads, swap creatives, or A/B test in seconds.
7. Geographic Reach Physically bounded to local print circulation, cable footprint, or road traffic. Borderless global reach by default, or tightly geofenced to a 2 km store radius.
8. Personalization & Retargeting Static, identical message displayed to every viewer simultaneously. Dynamic personalization (e.g., showing exact abandoned cart item via retargeting).

3. Practical Application & Key Takeaways

  • Real-World Hybrid Strategy ("Phygital"): Major Indian brands bridge both worlds — e.g., Cred or Swiggy placing a scannable UTM-tagged QR code on IPL billboards or newspaper front pages so offline impressions feed directly into measurable digital app installs.
  • Exam Tip: In 5-mark SPPU comparison questions, writing 8 crisp, parallel rows with concrete Indian metrics (e.g., TRP vs GA4, Rs. 100/day vs print slot cost) reliably earns full marks.

The Indian Digital Ecosystem is uniquely shaped by mobile-first leapfrogging (skipping desktop PCs straight to smartphones) and open Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI), transforming over 800M+ citizens across Tier-1, Tier-2, and Tier-3 towns into active digital consumers and instant UPI transactors.

1. Definition / Core Principle

The Digital Marketing Funnel is a sequential customer-journey model illustrating how a broad pool of unknown prospects narrows stage-by-stage through Awareness, Consideration, Conversion, and Retention into loyal, repeat buyers and brand advocates.

2. Neat Labelled Diagram & Exam Explanation

  • Advocacy Loop (Left Dashed Arrow): Happy Retention customers share reviews and referral links, feeding free Earned Awareness back into the top of the funnel.

Funnel Stage Buyer Mindset Primary Content / Touchpoints Drop-Off Cause & Recovery Tactic
1. Awareness "I have a need or curiosity." Instagram Reels, YouTube explainer shorts, SEO guides. Cause: Weak hook. Fix: Test first-3-second visual hooks & vernacular captions.

Q1.5 Explain the RACE Strategic Planning Framework (Reach, Act, Convert, Engage) with a neat diagram, KPIs per stage, and an Indian brand case study. 7 Marks • High-Frequency SPPU Q

1. Definition / Core Principle

Created by Dave Chaffey (Smart Insights), the RACE Framework is a practical, customer-lifecycle digital marketing planning system covering four active stages — Reach, Act, Convert, and Engage — built upon an initial Plan foundation to align digital channels directly with measurable business KPIs.

2. Neat Labelled Diagram & Exam Explanation

Diagram Explanation (How to Describe in Exam)
  • Plan Foundation: Before running ads, define SMART objectives, target personas, and GA4/Pixel attribution.
  • Linear Progression (Reach → Act → Convert): Moves strangers from initial discovery on external platforms (Reach) to interacting with Owned content (Act) and completing commercial transactions (Convert).
  • Engage Feedback Loop: Post-purchase onboarding and advocacy loops generate Earned referrals back into Reach, reducing long-term CAC.

3. Stage-By-Stage RACE Breakdown Table

RACE Stage Core Objective Key Digital Marketing Activities Measurable KPIs
Reach Grow brand visibility & drive traffic to Owned media. Organic SEO, Google Display, YouTube Pre-roll, Influencer PR. Impressions, Unique Reach, Organic Traffic, Share of Voice.
Act (Interact) Prompt visitor interaction, exploration, or lead capture. High-speed landing pages, interactive calculators, lead magnets, blogs. Click-Through Rate (CTR), Engagement Rate, Time on Page, MQLs.
Convert Turn warm leads into paying online or offline customers. CRO (A/B testing), retargeting ads, UPI/card 1-click checkout. Conversion Rate (CR%), Cost Per Acquisition (CPA), AOV, ROAS.
Engage Maximize Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) & referrals. Automated Email/WhatsApp drips, loyalty programs, review requests. Repeat Purchase Rate, Retention %, LTV, Net Promoter Score (NPS).

def build_utm_url(base_url, source, medium, campaign, content=None):
    params = {
        "utm_source": source.lower().strip(),
        "utm_medium": medium.lower().strip(),
        "utm_campaign": campaign.lower().strip(),

    return f"{base_url}?{urlencode(params)}"

Q2.1 (Solved KPI Numerical 1) An EdTech startup runs a Google Search campaign with a spend of Rs. 30,000. It records 1,20,000 impressions, 3,000 clicks, 1,050 single-page bounces, and 120 course enrollments. State formulas and compute: (a) CTR, (b) CPC, (c) CPM, (d) Conversion Rate, and (e) Bounce Rate & Engagement Rate. 6 Marks • Guaranteed Numerical
SPPU Marking Split: Core Formula Box (1.5M) · Step-by-Step Substitutions & Answers (3.5M) · Campaign Diagnostic & Takeaways (1M)

1. Definition / Formula Box

Performance Marketing KPIs quantify top-of-funnel ad efficiency (CTR, CPM), click cost efficiency (CPC), and post-click landing-page effectiveness (Conversion Rate and Bounce Rate).

Unit 2 Core Performance Formulas (SPPU Formula Sheet)
  • $\text{CTR (\%)} = \left(\dfrac{\text{Total Clicks}}{\text{Total Impressions}}\right) \times 100$
  • $\text{CPC} = \dfrac{\text{Total Ad Spend}}{\text{Total Clicks}}$
  • $\text{CPM (Cost Per Mille)} = \left(\dfrac{\text{Total Ad Spend}}{\text{Total Impressions}}\right) \times 1{,}000$
  • $\text{Conversion Rate (CR \%)} = \left(\dfrac{\text{Total Conversions}}{\text{Total Clicks}}\right) \times 100$
  • $\text{Bounce Rate (\%)} = \left(\dfrac{\text{Single-Page Unengaged Sessions}}{\text{Total Sessions / Clicks}}\right) \times 100, \quad \text{Engagement Rate} = 100\% - \text{Bounce Rate}$

2. Given Data & Step-by-Step Numerical Solution

Given Data: $\text{Total Ad Spend} = \text{Rs.\ } 30{,}000$, $\text{Impressions} = 1{,}20{,}000$, $\text{Clicks} = 3{,}000$, $\text{Bounced Sessions} = 1{,}050$, $\text{Conversions} = 120$.

  1. (a) Click-Through Rate (CTR): $$\text{CTR} = \frac{3{,}000}{1{,}20{,}000} \times 100 = 0.025 \times 100 = \mathbf{2.50\%}$$
  2. (b) Cost Per Click (CPC): $$\text{CPC} = \frac{30{,}000}{3{,}000} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 10.00 \text{ per click}}$$
  3. (c) Cost Per Mille (CPM — per 1,000 impressions): $$\text{CPM} = \left(\frac{30{,}000}{1{,}20{,}000}\right) \times 1{,}000 = 0.25 \times 1{,}000 = \mathbf{Rs.\ 250.00}$$
  4. (d) Conversion Rate (CR): $$\text{CR} = \frac{120}{3{,}000} \times 100 = 0.04 \times 100 = \mathbf{4.00\%}$$
  5. (e) Bounce Rate & GA4 Engagement Rate: $$\text{Bounce Rate} = \frac{1{,}050}{3{,}000} \times 100 = \mathbf{35.00\%}$$ $$\text{Engagement Rate} = 100\% - 35.00\% = \mathbf{65.00\%}$$

Q2.2 (Solved KPI Numerical 2) Differentiate ROAS vs. ROI. A Pune D2C footwear brand spends Rs. 40,000 on Instagram Ads and Rs. 10,000 on sales/creative overhead. The campaign generates 80 orders at Rs. 2,500 each (Rs. 2,00,000 revenue) with COGS + shipping of Rs. 1,000 per pair. Out of 80 orders, 60 are first-time buyers whose average gross-margin LTV is Rs. 3,000. Compute CPA, ROAS, Net ROI%, CAC, and LTV:CAC ratio. 7 Marks • High-Weightage Numerical
SPPU Marking Split: Formula Box & ROAS vs ROI Warning (2M) · Step-by-Step Numerical Solution (3.5M) · Unit Economics Verdict & Takeaways (1.5M)

1. Core Formulas & ROAS vs. ROI Distinction

Unit Economics Metrics evaluate whether customer acquisition generates sustainable net business profit after accounting for ad spend, product cost of goods sold (COGS), and sales overhead.

Acquisition & Profitability Formulas
  • $\text{CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)} = \dfrac{\text{Total Ad Spend}}{\text{Total Conversions (Orders)}}$
  • $\text{ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)} = \dfrac{\text{Revenue Attributed to Ads}}{\text{Total Ad Spend}}$
  • $\text{Net ROI (\%)} = \left(\dfrac{\text{Total Revenue} - \text{Total Cost}}{\text{Total Cost}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\dfrac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Total Cost}}\right) \times 100$
  • $\text{CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)} = \dfrac{\text{Marketing Spend} + \text{Sales Overhead}}{\text{New Customers Acquired}}$
  • $\text{LTV : CAC Ratio} = \dfrac{\text{Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)}}{\text{CAC}} \quad (\text{Healthy Benchmark: } \ge 3 : 1)$
Critical Exam Distinction: ROAS vs. ROI

ROAS is a top-line media metric comparing gross revenue solely against ad spend ($\text{Revenue} / \text{Ad Spend}$). ROI (%) is a bottom-line business profitability metric that deducts COGS, shipping, and team overhead from revenue before dividing by total investment. A campaign can show 3x ROAS and still lose money if COGS exceeds 67%!

2. Step-by-Step Numerical Solution

Given Data: $\text{Ad Spend} = \text{Rs.\ } 40{,}000$, $\text{Sales/Creative Overhead} = \text{Rs.\ } 10{,}000$, $\text{Orders} = 80$, $\text{AOV} = \text{Rs.\ } 2{,}500$, $\text{COGS/pair} = \text{Rs.\ } 1{,}000$, $\text{New Customers} = 60$, $\text{LTV} = \text{Rs.\ } 3{,}000$.

  1. Cost Per Acquisition (CPA per order): $$\text{CPA} = \frac{40{,}000}{80} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 500.00 \text{ per order}}$$
  2. Total Ad Revenue & Return on Ad Spend (ROAS): $$\text{Revenue} = 80 \times 2{,}500 = \text{Rs.\ } 2{,}00{,}000$$ $$\text{ROAS} = \frac{2{,}00{,}000}{40{,}000} = \mathbf{5.00\text{x}} \text{ (or } \mathbf{500\%}\text{)}$$
  3. Total Cost, Net Profit & Net ROI (%): $$\text{Total COGS} = 80 \times 1{,}000 = \text{Rs.\ } 80{,}000$$ $$\text{Total Investment Cost} = 40{,}000 \text{ (Ads)} + 10{,}000 \text{ (Sales)} + 80{,}000 \text{ (COGS)} = \text{Rs.\ } 1{,}30{,}000$$ $$\text{Net Profit} = 2{,}00{,}000 - 1{,}30{,}000 = \text{Rs.\ } 70{,}000$$ $$\text{ROI (\%)} = \left(\frac{70{,}000}{1{,}30{,}000}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{53.85\%}$$
  4. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC — New Customers Only): $$\text{CAC} = \frac{40{,}000 + 10{,}000}{60} = \frac{50{,}000}{60} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 833.33 \text{ per new customer}}$$
  5. LTV : CAC Ratio: $$\text{LTV : CAC} = \frac{3{,}000}{833.33} = \mathbf{3.60 : 1}$$

3. Python Verification & Unit Economics Verdict

ad_spend, sales_cost = 40000, 10000
orders, aov, cogs_unit = 80, 2500, 1000
new_cust, ltv = 60, 3000

revenue = orders * aov
total_cost = ad_spend + sales_cost + (orders * cogs_unit)
cpa = ad_spend / orders
roas = revenue / ad_spend
roi_pct = ((revenue - total_cost) / total_cost) * 100
cac = (ad_spend + sales_cost) / new_cust
ltv_cac = ltv / cac
print(f"CPA=Rs.{cpa:.2f}, ROAS={roas:.2f}x, ROI={roi_pct:.2f}%, CAC=Rs.{cac:.2f}, LTV:CAC={ltv_cac:.2f}:1")
  • Sustainability Verdict: Since $\text{LTV:CAC} = 3.60:1 \ge 3:1$ benchmark and Net ROI is $+53.85\%$, the campaign is both profitably scalable and capital-efficient.

On-Page Element Optimal Specification Best Practice Common Mistake to Avoid

3. Valid On-Page SEO Head & JSON-LD Schema Code

<head>
  <meta charset="UTF-8">
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">
  <title>Best Python Course in Pune | SPPU Certified Bootcamp</title>
  <meta name="description" content="Learn Python & Data Structures in Pune with live projects, SPPU exam prep, and placement support. Enroll today!">
  <link rel="canonical" href="https://example.edu.in/courses/python-pune">

  <!-- JSON-LD Structured Data (Schema.org) for Google Rich Snippets -->
  <script type="application/ld+json">
  {
    "@context": "https://schema.org",
    "@type": "Course",
    "name": "Python & Data Structures Bootcamp",
    "description": "Hands-on Python programming and SPPU SE Computer exam preparation.",
    "provider": {
      "@type": "Organization",
      "name": "Pune Tech Academy",
      "sameAs": "https://example.edu.in"
    }
  }
  </script>
</head>

4. Key Takeaways

  • Why JSON-LD Wins: Google explicitly recommends JSON-LD over inline Microdata because it separates semantic entity markup from messy HTML layout tags.
  • Canonical Protection: Adding a self-referencing canonical tag prevents URL tracking parameters (?utm_source=... or ?sort=price) from splitting search ranking equity across duplicate pages.

1. Definition / Core Principle

Technical SEO optimizes server infrastructure, crawl directives, rendering speed, and page stability so search bots can crawl and index content effortlessly. Diagram Explanation (How to Describe in Exam)
  • LCP (≤ 2.5s): Measures how fast the main hero image or headline block paints in the viewport.

3. robots.txt vs. XML Sitemap vs. noindex Table & Config Snippets

Technical File / Tag Location Primary Purpose Critical Behavior Note
robots.txt Root directory (/robots.txt) Controls Crawling — tells bots which directories (e.g., /admin/, /cart/) to skip to save Crawl Budget. Does NOT guarantee de-indexing if an external site links to a disallowed URL!
sitemap.xml Root / GSC submitted URL Facilitates Discovery — lists all canonical, index-worthy URLs and <lastmod> timestamps. Only include HTTP 200 canonical URLs; exclude 404/redirect/noindex pages.
<meta name="robots" content="noindex"> Inside HTML <head> Controls Indexing — instructs Googlebot to drop the page from Search Index. Bot must be allowed to crawl the page first in order to read the noindex tag!
